\section{Conclusion}
\label{conclusion}

\textbf{Main result.} Across five saved grouped partitions, structured TF-IDF logistic regression
has the highest mean test AP, $0.675 \pm 0.138$. Text-only LR remains close at $0.657 \pm 0.124$, and
structured fields improve test AP on only three of five partitions, so they are a small,
split-dependent addition rather than a uniform improvement.

\textbf{Neural result.} Full text and full fused fine-tuning reach mean AP $0.615$ and $0.597$; both
lose four of five paired test-AP comparisons to structured LR.

\textbf{Adaptation and fusion.} Full fine-tuning improves mean AP over top-two tuning by 0.106 for
text and 0.037 for fused inputs, on five and four partitions, the most consistent neural effect
found. Late structured fusion helps top-two tuning ($+0.050$ AP, four partitions) but not full tuning
($-0.018$, two partitions). Explanations of the fully tuned model point to the company profile and to
clerical-work and earnings vocabulary.

\textbf{Methodological result.} Randomized related-ad grouping, training-local preprocessing, a
majority floor, validation-selected epochs and thresholds, saved row scores and paired contrasts
expose variability that headline accuracy would hide. The seed-2 prevalence dip, group
concentration and truncation audit further show why partition composition and field survival must
accompany aggregate metrics.

\textbf{Next step.} A stronger evaluation should freeze the analysis, repeat several optimization
seeds within each saved partition, and then evaluate once on an untouched external or
time/source-split dataset with a prespecified operating rule. Until then, the findings remain
exploratory and carry no statistical-significance, equivalence or deployment-readiness claim.
