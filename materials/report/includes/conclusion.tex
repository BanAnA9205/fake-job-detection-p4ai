\section{Conclusion}
\label{conclusion}

\textbf{Main result.} Across five saved grouped partitions, structured TF-IDF logistic regression
has the highest mean test AP, $0.5928 \pm 0.1651$. Text-only LR remains competitive at
$0.5762 \pm 0.1875$ AP and has higher mean F1, 0.5301 versus 0.4633. Structured fields are thus a
small, split-dependent ranking addition rather than a uniform improvement.

\textbf{Neural result.} Full text and full fused achieve mean AP $0.4941$ and $0.5047$, but both lose
all five paired test-AP comparisons to structured LR.

\textbf{Adaptation and fusion.} Full fine-tuning improves mean AP over top-two tuning by only 0.0245
for text and 0.0474 for fused inputs, with positive differences on two and three partitions. Late
structured fusion is unstable across validation and test, including a full-fused-minus-text mean
test-AP change of $+0.0106$ on only two positive partitions. Neither more trainable parameters nor
fusion produces a demonstrated neural winner.

\textbf{Methodological result.} Exact related-ad grouping, training-local preprocessing, a majority
floor, validation-selected epochs and thresholds, saved row scores and paired contrasts expose
variability that headline accuracy would hide. The seed-2 concentration and truncation audit further
show why group composition and field survival must accompany aggregate metrics.

\textbf{Next step.} A stronger evaluation should freeze the analysis, repeat several optimization
seeds within each saved partition, and then evaluate once on an untouched external or
time/source-split dataset with a prespecified operating rule. Until then, the findings remain
exploratory and carry no statistical-significance, equivalence or deployment-readiness claim.
